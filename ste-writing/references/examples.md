# Non-STE to STE: Worked Examples

> These examples are original. They show how to change common technical text
> into STE. Each example shows the Non-STE text, the STE text, and the reason
> for the change.

## Example 1: A procedure

**Non-STE**

> Prior to commencing the inspection, it should be ensured that the equipment
> has been de-energized and that all of the personnel have vacated the area,
> after which the technician may proceed to carefully remove the access panel
> while taking care not to damage the surrounding structure.

**STE**

> **Before you start the inspection:**
>
> 1. Set the equipment to OFF.
> 2. Make sure that all persons are out of the area.
> 3. Remove the access panel.
> 4. Be careful. Do not cause damage to the structure around the panel.

**Reasons for the change**

- The sentence had 48 words. STE limits a procedure sentence to 20 words.
- The sentence had many actions. STE puts one action in each step.
- "Prior to commencing" became "Before you start". STE uses approved words.
- "it should be ensured" (passive) became "Make sure that" (active,
  imperative).
- "de-energized" became "Set to OFF". STE gives a clear, direct action.

## Example 2: A description

**Non-STE**

> The cooling system, which is comprised of a pump, a reservoir, and a series
> of interconnected lines that are routed throughout the assembly, is
> responsible for the dissipation of the thermal energy that is generated
> during operation.

**STE**

> The cooling system removes heat during operation. The system has these
> components:
>
> - a pump
> - a reservoir
> - the lines that connect the components

**Reasons for the change**

- The sentence had more than 25 words. STE limits a descriptive sentence to
  25 words.
- "is comprised of" and "is responsible for the dissipation of" are complex.
  STE uses simple verbs: "has" and "removes".
- The list of components is now a vertical list. A vertical list is easier to
  read.
- "thermal energy" became "heat". STE prefers the simple, common word.

## Example 3: A warning

**Non-STE**

> Caution should be exercised during this step, as the residual pressure in
> the accumulator can cause the fluid to be ejected at high velocity, possibly
> resulting in injury.

**STE**

> **WARNING:** THE ACCUMULATOR IS UNDER PRESSURE. FLUID CAN COME OUT AT HIGH
> SPEED. YOU CAN GET AN INJURY. RELEASE THE PRESSURE BEFORE YOU DO THIS STEP.

**Reasons for the change**

- The warning comes before the step. The reader sees the risk first.
- The warning starts with a clear condition and a clear command.
- It tells the risk (fluid, injury) and how to prevent the risk (release the
  pressure).
- Short sentences replace one long sentence.

## Example 4: Sentence-level fixes

| Non-STE | STE | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| The unit is to be tested. | Do a test of the unit. | "test" is a noun; use the active imperative |
| Rotate the valve until it is closed. | Turn the valve until it is closed. | "rotate" is not approved; use "turn" |
| The bearing should be lubricated periodically. | Lubricate the bearing at the specified intervals. | active voice; specific, not vague |
| Utilize the correct tool to remove the fastener. | Use the correct tool to remove the fastener. | "utilize" is not approved; use "use" |
| The lamp will illuminate when the test is successful. | The lamp comes on if the test is satisfactory. | simple present; approved words |

## How to read these examples

The STE text is not the only correct answer. Frequently, you can use different
approved words and keep the same meaning. The reasons show the rule behind
each change. Learn the reason, not only the result.
