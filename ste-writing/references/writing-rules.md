> section titles and rule numbers below are from Issue 9 (2025-01-15).

## Section 1 - Words

The word rules control the vocabulary. They are the center of STE.

- Use only approved words from the dictionary. An approved word is a word with
  an approved meaning in the dictionary.
- Use each approved word as one part of speech only. For example, use "test"
  as a noun, not as a verb.
- Use each approved word with one approved meaning only.
- Use approved technical nouns for parts, tools, and materials (Rule 1.5). The
  dictionary does not list technical nouns.
- Use approved technical verbs for technical actions (Rule 1.12), for example
  "to drill" or "to ream".

> **Note:** Issue 9 uses the term "technical noun". It replaces the term
> "technical name" from earlier issues.

**Non-STE:** Oil the bearing.
**STE:** Lubricate the bearing with oil.

The word "oil" is a noun. Do not use it as a verb.

## Section 2 - Multi-word nouns

A long string of nouns is difficult to read. The reader cannot see which noun
is the head noun. The rules keep multi-word nouns short and clear.

- Do not make a multi-word noun of more than 3 words.
- If a multi-word noun is not clear, divide it and use prepositions.
- Give a definition the first time you use a new multi-word noun.

**Non-STE:** the runway light connection resistance calibration
**STE:** the calibration of the resistance for the connection of the runway
lights

The STE version is longer. But the reader can see the relation between the
words.

## Section 3 - Verbs

The verb rules control tense, voice, and form. They remove ambiguous and
complex verb constructions.

- Use only these verb forms:
  - the infinitive (to install)
  - the imperative (Install the bolt.)
  - the simple present (The valve opens.)
  - the simple past (The system stopped.)
  - the simple future (The lamp will come on.)
  - the past participle, as an adjective only (the connected cable)
- Use the active voice (Rule 3.6).
- In descriptive writing, use the passive voice only if the agent is not known
  or not necessary.
- Do not use the "-ing" form as a verb. Use it as a noun or a modifier only,
  for example "the landing gear".
- Do not make complex verb tenses with auxiliary verbs, for example "had been
  removed" or "is being tested".

**Non-STE:** The filter should be replaced if it is found to be contaminated.
**STE:** Replace the filter if it is dirty.

## Section 4 - Sentences

The sentence rules keep one idea in each sentence and control the structure.

- Write one instruction or one idea in each sentence.
- Use vertical lists for complex information (Rule 4.3).
- Do not remove words to make a sentence short. Keep the subject and the
  verb. Use an article (the, a, an) or a demonstrative adjective (this,
  these) before a noun (Rule 4.5).
- Use connecting words to make the relation between ideas clear.

> **Note:** The maximum sentence length is not in this section. The word-count
> limits are in Section 5 (procedures) and Section 6 (descriptions).

**Non-STE:** Remove the panel, disconnect the two cables, and put the panel in
a safe area while making sure not to damage the seal.
**STE:**

1. Remove the panel.
2. Disconnect the two cables.
3. Put the panel in a safe area.
4. Make sure that you do not cause damage to the seal.

## Section 5 - Procedural writing

A procedure tells the reader to do something. These rules make each step clear
and direct.

- Write no more than 20 words in a procedural sentence (Rule 5.1).
- Start each instruction with the imperative (the command form of the verb).
- Write one instruction in each step. Write two steps for two actions.
- Put the steps in the correct sequence.
- Use a key word ("If", "Before", "After") to show a condition.

**Non-STE:** The technician will then proceed to carefully tighten all of the
fasteners.
**STE:** Tighten all the fasteners.

---

## Section 6 - Descriptive writing

A description tells the reader about a thing or a system. These rules keep
descriptions clear, but they permit more than procedures do.

- Write no more than 25 words in a descriptive sentence (Rule 6.3).
- Use the active voice when you can. Use the passive voice only if the agent
  is not known.
- Keep to one topic in each paragraph.
- Use the simple verb tenses.
- Use paragraphs and headings to divide a long description.

that are interconnected in a complex manner, serves to actuate the flight
control surfaces.
**STE:** The hydraulic system moves the flight control surfaces. It has these

## Section 7 - Safety instructions

A warning tells the reader about a risk of injury or death. A caution tells
the reader about a risk of damage. These rules put the risk before the step.

- Put the warning or the caution before the step, not after it.
- Start a safety instruction with a clear command or a clear condition.
- Tell the reader the risk. Tell the reader how to prevent the risk.
- Keep the safety text short and specific.

**Non-STE:** Care should be taken as the surface may be hot.
**STE:**

> **WARNING:** THE SURFACE IS HOT. DO NOT TOUCH THE SURFACE. YOU CAN GET
> BURNS.

---

## Section 8 - Punctuation and word count

These rules control the punctuation marks and the numbers. Clear punctuation
helps the reader to parse a sentence.

- Use simple punctuation. Do not use complex or unusual marks.
- Do not use the slash (/) to mean "and" or "or". Write the word.
- Use numerals for numbers, for example "3", not "three".
- Write units in a consistent form.

**Non-STE:** Check the oil/fluid level in three places.
**STE:** Check the oil level and the fluid level in 3 places.

---

## Section 9 - Writing practices

These rules control the layout and the presentation. Good layout helps the
reader to find and use the information.

- Use a vertical list for parallel items or steps.
- Use a table for data with two or more dimensions.
- Use headings to show the structure.
- Do not use different words for the same idea. Use the same word each time.
- Do not use a synonym for style. Consistency is more important than variety.

**Non-STE:** First remove the cover. Next, take off the shroud. Then detach
the housing.
**STE:** (Use one verb for one action.)

1. Remove the cover.
2. Remove the shroud.
3. Remove the housing.

---

## Summary

The word rules (Section 1) and the verb rules (Section 3) give the largest
effect. Apply them first. Then check the sentence structure (Section 4), the
word count (Sections 5 and 6), the safety order (Section 7), and the layout
(Section 9). Last, replace each unapproved word with an approved word from
`dictionary.md`. If you are not sure, go to the official document.
