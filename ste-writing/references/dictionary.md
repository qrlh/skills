# The STE Dictionary

> This file explains how the STE dictionary works and gives a small,
> illustrative set of substitutions. It does not reproduce the official
> ~900-word dictionary. For the authoritative word list, get the free
> official copy from https://asd-ste100.org.

## How the dictionary works

The dictionary is Part 2 of STE. It lists approved words and unapproved words.
Each entry has four columns:

1. **Word (part of speech).** The word and its permitted part of speech. An
   approved word is in UPPERCASE. An unapproved word is in lowercase.
2. **Approved meaning / ALTERNATIVES.** For an approved word, this column
   gives the one approved meaning. For an unapproved word, it gives approved
   words that you can use instead.
3. **STE example.** A sentence that shows correct use.
4. **Non-STE example.** A sentence that shows the common, incorrect use.

> The text in the definitions is not written in STE. STE controls the
> documentation that a writer produces, not the dictionary's own definitions.

## The core principle

**One word, one part of speech, one meaning.**

- A word is approved for one part of speech only. Example: "test" is approved
  as a noun, not as a verb. Do not write "test the unit". Write "do a test of
  the unit".
- A word is approved for one meaning only. Example: the verb "close" is
  approved to mean "to move together" or "to operate a circuit breaker". It is
  not approved to mean "to end a meeting".
- If a word or a meaning is not in the dictionary, you cannot use it. Find an
  approved alternative.

## Technical nouns and technical verbs

The dictionary does not list every technical word. You can use a technical
noun or a technical verb if it names a real part, tool, material, or action.

- Technical nouns: "propeller", "overhead panel", "aural warning system".
- Technical verbs: "to drill", "to ream", "to solder".

The word rules in Section 1 control how to use technical nouns and technical
verbs. Rule 1.5 covers technical nouns. Rule 1.12 covers technical verbs. Keep
them short and specific.

> **Note:** Issue 9 uses the term "technical noun". It replaces the older term
> "technical name".

### Words that look unapproved but are approved

Do not assume that a formal or a long word is unapproved. Some words that
writers frequently replace are approved words in STE. For example, these words
are approved: APPROXIMATELY, ADJACENT, SUFFICIENT, ILLUMINATE, and
DE-ENERGIZE. Always check the dictionary before you replace a word.

## How to replace a word

Do these steps for each unapproved word.

1. Find the word in the dictionary.
2. Read the approved alternatives in column 2.
3. Choose the alternative that keeps your meaning.
4. Change the sentence if the new word needs a different construction.
5. Read the sentence again. Make sure that the meaning is correct.

> The alternatives are suggestions. Frequently, you can use a different
> approved word or a different construction and keep the same meaning.

## When you are not sure about a word

The substitution table above is a small example set. It is not the authority.
The official dictionary (Part 2) is the authority. Are you not sure that a
word is approved? Confirm it in the official dictionary. See
`official-source.md`.
